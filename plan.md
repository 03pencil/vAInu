# Project Plan

## The problem

Many pumps, on ships and in factories, have no modern monitoring. Cheap sensors can be added to them later (this is called *retrofit*). The goal is to turn that sensor data into an early-warning assistant. It should spot developing faults, explain why it thinks so, and suggest what a person should check.

The challenge is **RETROFIT AI — Make Existing Machines Intelligent** (SinceAI 2026, Turku). The organizer rules are kept outside this repo: `CHALLENGE_BRIEF.md`, `BENCHMARK_RULES.md`, `TECHNICAL_MODEL.md`, and `RETROFIT_FEASIBILITY.md`.

## Rules we design around

- **Read-only.** We never write to the pump or its controls.
- **Advisory only.** Alerts suggest. People decide.
- **Be honest.** Say `UNKNOWN` when the cause is unclear. Make no safety or certification claims.
- **Sensor or machine?** A broken sensor must not look like a broken pump. A drifting sensor is flagged as suspect, not as a machine fault.
- **Different pumps, different sensors.** Pumps are grouped into tiers by what they can measure:
  - T0 (legacy): basic sensors only, no vibration.
  - T1 (retrofit): vibration and temperature update only every 300 s, so short events can hide between samples.
  - T2 (full): sees almost everything.
- **Normal changes are not faults.** Start-ups, load changes, heavy weather, and voltage dips are benign and must not cause alerts.
- **Few false alarms.** A low-confidence `UNKNOWN` is better than a confident wrong answer.
- **No peeking.** An alert at time *t* may only use data up to *t*.

## How the predictions work

The system has three layers. Each one is simpler to check than the next.

1. **Math (deterministic).** Learns a healthy range for each pump and operating mode. Checks physics, such as the pump curve, efficiency, and temperature lag. Estimates the time until a limit is reached, for example "vibration hits alarm in about 38 h". *Mostly done.*
2. **Small learned detector.** An anomaly score and a fault classifier, written with numpy only. *Planned.*
3. **SmolLM narration.** Turns the math result into a plain-language explanation. It never changes the alarm status and never does the numbers itself. *Planned.*

Finally, every prediction is logged and compared with what really happened, so the system can be recalibrated over time. *Planned.*

## Hardware

Short list. Details are in [`bom.md`](bom.md).

- Arduino UNO Q (provided by Majos Hacking League). Use the 4 GB version (ABX00173) for SmolLM.
- A 5 V / 3 A USB-C wall adapter for power. Do not power it from a PC port.
- A USB-C data cable for one-time setup from a PC.
- Wi-Fi, which the board uses for the dashboard and the model download.
- Three wires (TX, RX, GND) for the serial link from the logic box to the board.

## Scoring

The benchmark gives a composite score from 0 to 100:

```
composite = 100 × (0.35 F1 + 0.20 timeliness + 0.25 fault-type recall
                   + 0.10 class recall + 0.10 alert burden)
```

- Baseline B1 (mode-aware z-score) scores **78.0** on validation. **Our target is to beat it.**
- Alerts less than 5 minutes apart on one asset merge into one burst.
- More than one reminder per 12 hours counts as a false alert.

Judging criteria and their weights:

| Criterion | Weight | How vAInu addresses it |
|---|---|---|
| Business usefulness | 25 % | Early warning time versus avoided stops. Alerts include evidence, a recommendation, and stated uncertainty. |
| Technical validity | 25 % | The composite score above, plus our own KPI gate. |
| Reliability and safety | 20 % | Sensor/machine separation, honest `UNKNOWN`, advisory only. |
| Retrofit feasibility | 20 % | Read-only, works offline, per-tier detection, reusable across rotating machines. |
| Working demo and clarity | 10 % | Simple dashboard with live values, predictions, and uncertainty. |

## Current numbers

Starter tier, measured on 2026-11-07:

- All 20 events detected, and 10 of 10 deadline events caught early.
- Median early-warning lead time: 1.6 h.
- Benign false-alarm rate: 13.8 %. **Target: under 2 %.**
